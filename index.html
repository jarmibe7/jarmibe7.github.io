About Me

Quality training data is limited.
So how can robots adapt?

Hi, I'm Jared.

Starting Fall 2026 I'll be a mechanical engineering PhD student at Northwestern University as a member of the Interactive and Emergent Autonomy Lab, led by Dr. Todd Murphey. In Spring 2026 I graduated from Northwestern with a BS and MS in Mechanical Engineering, thesis track, both concentrated in robotics.

For my MS thesis I developed a novel data collection method for online world model training, enabling robots to make accurate dynamics predictions with significantly less training data. Robots using this data collection algorithm also exhibited emergent exploratory behaviors, frequently performing long-horizon interactions with objects in the environment.

Right now, I want to investigate more strategies with which a robot can actively learn and reason about its environment. Robot training data is incredibly limited (at least when compared to LLMs), and the physical world also has a lot of edge cases. It's incredibly important for robots to be able quickly adapt and suceed in new environments if they're going to be useful across the board.

What I do

I specialize in building intelligent robotic systems that are capable of learning about and interacting with their environment using a combination of techniques from reinforcement learning, world models, computer vision, and control theory. Some of my projects have included:

  • Reinforcement learning for a high DOF dexterous robot
  • Sim-to-real optimization with state-of-the-art machine learning techniques
  • Simulation frameworks for RL and generative modeling applications
  • Lightweight predictive dynamics models
  • Control systems for robotic arms and mobile robots
  • ML model/architecture design for clinical research

Areas of interest

ACTIVE LEARNING / REINFORCEMENT LEARNING / WORLD MODELING / DEXTEROUS MANIPULATION / MEDICAL ROBOTICS + ML
